## 01 / Flip cockpit

Use case: product-mockup.
Create an audacious, photorealistic FRONT-FACING design presentation of a millennial car-audio CD music machine for JACKIE's black-paper portfolio. Landscape image. The attached image is ONLY a reference for black fibrous paper and distressed JACKIE typography. Completely REJECT its boring rectangular silver stereo with left knob and two big right buttons. Invent a much more spectacular original design, with authentic 2000-era Japanese high-end car audio design vocabulary.
Primary concept: SILVER FLIP-COCKPIT. Sculpted titanium-silver and satin chrome layered fascia, sharply cut asymmetrical shoulders, smoked glass upper instrumentation band, large CENTER-LOW rotary volume wheel with a deeply scalloped mechanical rim and polished inset hub. Two diagonal clusters of small translucent ICE BLUE physical keys flank the wheel, labeled only PLAY, NEXT, MUTE, DISPLAY. Upper display has multiple physically divided subwindows: small cyan segmented circular volume meter on left, wide warm lime-green fine dot-matrix spectrum and condensed song text across center, red-orange tiny playback indicator and ivory segmented time on right. This is 2001 display technology, colored VFD and LCD segments behind tinted glass, NOT a modern OLED touchscreen or generic sci-fi HUD. A thin hidden CD transport runs above screen band. Elaborately folded face geometry, blade-shaped raised button caps, tiny printed technical legends, beautiful asymmetry balanced by central wheel. Surprising, tactile and expensive, more Clarion/Pioneer motorized car stereo than domestic amplifier, original branding JACKIE only.
Composition: main large unit centered right, dead straight frontal camera, enough scale to see machining; huge cropped distressed offwhite JACKIE occupies far left background without covering object. Small header '01 / FLIP COCKPIT'. Below main unit a single smaller secondary SAME-device view in open-disc state: entire front fascia tipped forward on connected bottom hinges, upper internal disc slot visible, partial thin foreshortened silver CD arc. Chassis stays strictly frontal, no whole-object rotation, no exploding disconnected parts. Small caption 'OPEN / CHANGE / CLOSE'. This is one design plus its alternate state, not two units installed together.
Lighting/material: warm recessed shadows, sharp narrow cool specular strokes on metal facets, dark glass reflections, micro machining and subtle handled wear, cold translucent blue keycaps softly backlit. Green/amber/cyan confined to display and buttons; black paper remains black. High fidelity real geometry and premium product photography, not a flat vector, not uniform dirty beige, not noisy distressed texture sprayed over metal.
Avoid minimalist slabs, modern rounded smart speakers, RGB rings, synthwave magenta, holograms, neon borders around whole machine, decorative wire spaghetti, futuristic weapons, giant floating CDs, upper TV screen, people's faces, birthday numbers, outside manufacturer logos. Bold millennial hardware sophistication, not a tame recoloring of reference stereo.

## 02 / Night spectrum

Use case: product-mockup.
Generate a landscape photorealistic industrial design concept board for an original circa-2000 Japanese CAR AUDIO CD player in JACKIE's website. The attached image is BLACK PAPER AND TYPOGRAPHY reference only; DO NOT reuse its bland silver rectangular device. This proposal must be dramatically bolder, more hypnotic and authentic to period aftermarket car audio, nothing like a polite home stereo.
Concept '02 / NIGHT SPECTRUM'. A low wide black-glass music head unit with dense beautiful colored SEGMENTED instrumentation and an asymmetrical tactile interface. Fixed EXACT STRAIGHT FRONTAL CAMERA, no three-quarter angle. Main device large across right 70%, left cropped towering distressed offwhite JACKIE; continuous fine charcoal paper background with restrained cobalt printing.
Industrial design: sculpted glossy piano-black wraparound upper lens occupying two thirds of fascia height, small brushed titanium lower lip and blade-like silver end cheeks, shallow sculpted angular silhouette, not a plain box. A large CHROME ROTARY DIAL intrudes asymmetrically into the glass at far left; partly recessed, scalloped black rubber rim, intricate brushed circular face and one tiny lime indicator. Below dial a compact directional rocker and pair of translucent lime physical keys. Lower-right edge two small beautifully inset transport paddles labeled PLAY and NEXT; smaller DISPLAY button. No huge modern playback buttons.
Behind the deep smoked glass: breathtaking dense but precisely aligned circa-2000 multicolor VFD / passive LCD artwork. Main center-right has about 16 thin columns built from tiny luminous lime and amber segmented cells; gray-green inactive cells remain visible. A tiny red-orange peak register at top, narrow icy cyan dot-matrix song title 'EDM DETECTION MODE', large ivory-green seven-segment time '00:24' at upper right. Left-central stacked red, amber, green rectangular playback state indicators with only actual labels DISC, PLAY, VOL. One tiny cyan horizontal stereo waveform strip beneath the spectrum. NOT modern smooth glowing charts, touch UI, floating sci-fi graphics or neon RGB trim.
Main view shows PLAYING. Beneath it one wide detail strip divided into two small screen closeups labeled 'SPECTRUM' and 'SCOPE', showing alternate display graphics of this same real hardware screen; no extra full devices. These are suggested switchable visualizations of music, not different EQ presets. Small board footnote 'ONE DISPLAY / TWO VIEWS'. Original tiny JACKIE hardware mark only.

Lights bright enough to be exciting and saturated but tiny sharp segmented edges, realistic smoked glass reflections stay discernible. Hardware has mild hairline scratches and polished touched edges, not dusty ruin. Titanium trim gets narrow cool reflections. Rich dark shadows, tack sharp machining, real physically based materials. No rainbow halo, no magenta, no city, no cables, no third-party brands, no 1127, no fake military labels, no generic sci-fi console, no oversize TV, no stand, no flat SVG. Bolder than a generic retro receiver, plausibly produced in the millennium.
